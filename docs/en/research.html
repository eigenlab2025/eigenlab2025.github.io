EIGEN LAB / RESEARCH

Research

We develop mathematical foundations for uncertainty-aware prediction and decision-making. We study optimal policy structure, convergence of approximations, and finite-data guarantees, then connect these results to algorithms and evaluation with real data.

Shared foundations: probability and statistics × optimization × learning and control

01
Prediction and statistics

Prediction and statistical learning under uncertainty

We represent state- and environment-dependent uncertainty through conditional distributions, and compute probabilistic reachable sets and prediction intervals. Robust learning addresses noise and anomalous observations.

Questions we study

  • How reliably can limited observations describe the range of future states?
  • How does distribution-estimation error affect prediction reliability?
  • How do approximate solutions converge as more data become available?

Related research

Probabilistic reachable sets of stochastic nonlinear systems with contextual uncertaintiesSet-Valued Regression of Wind Power Curve
02
Optimization, learning, and control

Chance-constrained optimization and safe decisions

We design policies that address safety and performance together. Our work includes the structure of flipping-based policies, tractable approximation methods, and offline safe reinforcement learning that respects the support of existing data.

Questions we study

  • What structure can an optimal decision rule have under probabilistic safety constraints?
  • How can policies improve while accounting for model error and distribution shift?
  • Under which assumptions can safety and performance be guaranteed probabilistically?

Related research

Approximate Methods for Solving Chance-Constrained Linear Programs in Probability Measure SpaceFlipping-based Policy for Chance-Constrained Markov Decision ProcessesOffline Guarded Safe Reinforcement Learning for Medical Treatment Optimization Strategies
03
Personalization and adaptation

Adaptive intelligence for people and systems

System identification and network re-stabilization with high-dimensional, limited data support our work on personalized prediction and intervention. We connect human understanding, digital twins, and privacy-preserving learning to human–AI collaboration.

Questions we study

  • How can sparse observations reveal early changes and useful intervention targets?
  • What should be shared across individuals and environments, and what should be learned separately?
  • How should observation timing, intervention timing, and collaboration be designed?

Related research

Re-Stabilizing Large-Scale Network Systems Using High-Dimension Low-Sample-Size Data AnalysisPrivacy-preserving federated learning via secret sharing and multi-key homomorphic encryption
